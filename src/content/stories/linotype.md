Before computers, the news was set in metal, and this machine is where the metal was cast. The Linotype turned an operator’s keystrokes into a solid line of type, and its name says it all: a “line o’ type.”

The operator typed on the keyboard, and small brass molds called matrices dropped into line to spell out each row of text. Molten metal was poured against the line to cast a single slug, ready for the press. When the page was printed, the slugs could be melted down and cast again.

Newspapers, including small-town papers like the Melrose Beacon, depended on machines like this one, and a skilled operator could set a column of type far faster than anyone could place letters by hand. Beside the machine you will find trays of hand-set type, printing supplies and a framed photograph from its history.

Stop in and see how the news was made, one line at a time.
